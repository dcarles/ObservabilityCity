# Decision log

How each decision in the plan was reached, round by round, with Dan on 24 Oct 2026. The plan document has the final version of every decision. This log keeps the extra detail and reasoning that a builder may need. Entries are in the order they were made, so a later entry can replace an earlier one (for example, early notes about districts, a depot and shared-infra.yaml were replaced by islands per pillar, the hub and hub.yaml). When this log and the plan disagree, the plan wins.

## Round 1: visual language

Decisions Dan made on 2026-10-24, in the planning thread:
- Weather = incidents only. Metrics drive buildings, not the sky.
- Roads: service-to-depot spokes (Service Bus = central depot), walkways only for direct sync calls, entry paths from the edge for outside callers.
- Third parties: their own area, health inferred from errors and latency on our calls to them, with pedestrian walks to them.
- Demo scenario switcher: one button per scenario (healthy, DLQ, high traffic, slow, incident, many errors, down, third-party outage), and also a detailed custom mode (set values per service).
- Demo data: registry files for teams and systems loaded as normal. Only the data sources are faked, as a fake adapter.
- Incidents: Grafana IRM, details to be worked out later.
- Backend: .NET preferred (Dan's strength), unless there's a strong reason otherwise.
- Runs locally on a developer PC for now. No SSO. Hosting in Azure comes later.

- Rendering: 3D low-poly with Three.js/react-three-fiber. Frontend in TypeScript, backend ASP.NET Core + SignalR.
- DLQs: a loading dock and red skip beside each subscriber, in its team's district. The depot only shows a warning light.
- Team layout: districts ring the depot. Systems declare calls/publishes/subscribes by system id, team closeness is computed from those links, and layout.yaml can pin positions.

Plan doc (2026-10-24): https://claude.ai/artifact/9GzxXsugoyXUQ8NoVc5Rou. Phases: 1 demo with fake sources, 2 Grafana/Prometheus + Log Analytics, 3 Service Bus/DLQs, 4 IRM incidents + third parties, 5 trustworthy, 6 host and grow. Waiting on Dan's review before any code.

**Why:** these steer the phased build plan. **How to apply:** don't re-ask them, and build the plan on top of them.

Visual language review, one signal at a time (started 2026-10-24, Dan said the first table copied the brief too closely):
- 1 System: shape by role. Role is optional in YAML, a closed enum, inferred from links/telemetry when omitted (api = receives requests, worker = only messaging, api+messaging = tower with dock, db/cache from dependency type, cron must be declared). The drift report flags contradictions.
- 2 Team: island per team. A central hub island (platform-owned, shared-infra.yaml) holds Service Bus and shared DBs, caches and APIM. Shared = owner platform, or referenced by 2+ teams (with a warning). Mainland coast = outside callers; third parties on small offshore islands.
- 3 Messaging: Service Bus = port on the hub island. Cargo boats sail to each subscriber team's pier; backlog = crates stacking on the pier. Water = async, bridges = sync (walkers).
- 4 DLQ: red quarantine pen on the team pier fills with broken crates, a red lighthouse beam flashes when >0, plus a floating count badge ("DLQ 37"). The hub port shows the town-wide total.
- 5 Traffic/capacity: floors = instance count on a doubling scale (1,2,3-4,5-8,9-16,17-32,33-64,65+ = 8 floors, the cap), exact count on a plaque. Lit windows = load (rps). Unhealthy instances = dark or cracked floors. Height changes are eased.
- 6 Errors: rooftop beacon (glowing bulb on a mast mid-roof, light pool on the roof), green/amber/red, red pulses, minimum on-screen size. Defaults 1% amber and 5% red, overridable per system. Windows weren't visible enough from afar for errors.
- 7 Latency/movement: number of walkers/boats = traffic, their speed = latency, a queue at the door/pier grows when p99 > threshold, and the crowd turns angry (stamping, amber "!" bubble, never red) when over SLA (slaP99Ms per system, default 1 s). Red stays reserved for errors.
- 8 Down: blackout, grey, barriers on the bridge, walkers turn back, a "Closed for repairs" sign and a repair van. Partial: the share of dark/cracked floors (top down) = the share of unhealthy instances, min 1 floor if any are unhealthy, plaque "5/10 healthy". Stale = grey, never healthy.
- 9 Incident: weather over the assigned team's island only (IRM assigns incidents to teams). sev4 light cloud, sev3 overcast, sev2 rain, sev1 storm with lightning. Worst severity wins, plus a count on the island sign. The rest of the town stays sunny. Platform incidents go over the hub. irmTeam is set in the team YAML. No building marker for now.
- 10 Outside callers: an airport on the map edge (plane rate = total inbound, arrivals board shows req/s). Passengers walk over main bridges only to islands that have external systems. Config: team-level `external: true` default with per-system override. Queue and anger show at the bridge end.
- 11 Sync calls (proposed mix, Dan's company is hub and spoke via APIM): each island has one bridge to the hub, with APIM as a gateway building on the hub (with its own signals). Walkers carry a team-colour flag (palette without red/amber/green). A dependency overlay shows dashed arcs team to team, sized by volume. Calls that bypass APIM get a direct bridge, which flags rule breaks. Data from APIM gateway logs or traces.
- 12 Third parties: small offshore islands with a direct bridge from each calling island. Same beacon/queue/closed rules, inferred from our outbound calls.
All 12 signals were written into the plan doc's visual language table on 2026-10-24. Dan didn't explicitly confirm the #11 hub-and-spoke mix (it was proposed, then he moved on).

## Round 2: architecture, config, sources, hub and messaging

Started 2026-10-24, after the visual language review (observability-city-plan). 14 topics: registry location, solution shape, live push, snapshot contract, adapter model, fake source/scenarios, local Azure auth, history storage, file layout/ownership, id naming, sources mapping, defaults/overrides, layout file, scenario files.

- 1 Registry lives in the app repo (registry/ folder). The loader takes a folder path, so it can move to a separate repo later.
- 2 Solution: .NET Aspire AppHost orchestrating the Host (API + SignalR), Core, Adapters.*, Contracts, and the web/ Vite frontend. azd to Azure Container Apps later.
- 3 Live push: SignalR (groups for per-team/log tail, two-way, Azure SignalR scale-out). The transport stays behind an interface, so it's swappable. Dan wasn't fully sure, so it's low stakes.
- 4 Contract: C# Snapshot records are the source of truth. The build emits openapi.json for /api/snapshot/latest, and openapi-typescript generates contracts.ts, so drift is a build error. Plus golden snapshot fixtures per scenario, used by both C# and TS renderer tests. (Dan was between this and hand-written types plus a contract test.)
- 5 Adapters: poll per source (own BackgroundService loop, interval, timeout, retry). One batched query for all systems per poll. A failure stales only that source. No webhooks while it runs on a dev PC.
- 6 Fake/scenarios: both. A generator adapter (healthy noisy baseline + scenario effects layered on top, sliders, can stand in for unwired systems) and a replay mode playing fixture timelines for tests and scripted demos (later reused for history replay).
- 7 Azure auth: DefaultAzureCredential (your own az/VS login locally, managed identity when hosted). Grafana/IRM tokens in dotnet user-secrets locally and Key Vault when hosted, never in YAML.
- 8 History: PARKED by Dan (2026-10-24). Live data only for the first versions. Later options: Cosmos everywhere (Aspire emulator, native TTL), SQLite, configurable retention (e.g. 90 days) with downsampling after 7 days (~5 GB vs ~23 GB full res).
- 9 Files: one file per pillar (registry/pillars/<pillar>.yaml) plus shared files (sources, shared-infra, messaging, third-parties, layout). Folder-per-team support can come later if a big team complains.
- Ownership data (Dan, 2026-10-24): the core credit platform (multi-tenant) has an Azure tag identifying the pillar/domain and a `pillar.code` telemetry attribute. Single-tenant parts of the estate may have neither. Plan: town.yaml `ownership: {azureTag: pillar, telemetryAttribute: pillar.code}`. Untagged systems go to a "Lost & found" island. Discovery writes drafts to registry/_discovered/ and never edits team files. A Registry health panel shows drift with fix lines.
- 10 Islands = PILLARS (not teams), one file per pillar, ids <pillarcode>.<system> e.g. crd.ledger-api, auto-placed from Azure tag and pillar.code. IRM teams map to pillars. Team districts within islands possible later. Validation: prefix must match the file, kebab-case, unique, aliases on rename, schema autocomplete, "did you mean".
- 11 Source mapping: convention + overrides. Default queries per source type on standard OTel metrics / App Insights tables joined by service name. A system adds a custom PromQL/KQL only when it's non-standard. Pillar default source, system can override.
- 12 Defaults: registry/defaults.yaml (app-wide per role: API 1 s, worker 30 s, DB 100 ms, errors 1%/5%, DLQ >0; same values baked into code as a last resort) -> pillar file "defaults" per role -> system override. Dan wants app-wide fallbacks when a pillar forgets its defaults. Example: an orchestrator API with an SLA of 2 s vs quick internal APIs at 300 ms. The drawer shows each effective value and its origin.
- 13 Layout: deterministic, no drag/pin. layout.yaml has islands.order (validated pillar ids; missing pillars appended with a warning). Ring around the hub clockwise from the top, auto radius. Above 12 islands, a staggered inner+outer ring (outer bridges pass between inner islands), perRing override. Dan expects 8-20 pillars. Buildings are gridded in pillar-file order. Fixed: hub centre, airport left, vendors outer right, Lost & found corner. Demo uses ~14 fake pillars.
- 14 Scenarios: registry/scenarios/*.yaml (caption + effects with target/effect/intensity, optional timeline), validated like the registry. Custom mode Save writes YAML, and the buttons are generated from the files.
All 14 topics written into the plan doc on 2026-10-24 (architecture decisions table + Registry config section rewritten for pillars).

Sources/hub/messaging/third-parties round (8 topics, 2026-10-24):
- S1 Environments: switchable, prod by default. sources.yaml has a section per environment (prod, uat, dev), a top-bar switcher, and a shared registry.
- S2 Binding: per-signal slots (metrics, instances, calls) with town-wide defaults. A pillar or system overrides only the slot that differs. Service Bus and IRM are bound globally.
- S3 Hub: its own file registry/hub.yaml (owner platform, irmTeams, resources with id hub.<name>, kind, azure resource id). Not a pillar (no systems). Health for every resource from Azure Resource Health. Richer signals per kind (APIM gateway logs -> rps/errors/p99; Service Bus server errors/throttling + port/DLQ totals). Pillars reference hub ids.
- Building kinds: registry/buildings.yaml catalog (id, displayName, model = built-in procedural model + params OR a .glb file, features: floors/pier/beacon). Roles and hub resource kinds map to these. Rename/restyle/extend via config.
- S4 Pillar-owned DB/cache (pillar file resources:, same format as hub) stays on the owner's island. sharedWith: [pillars] declares intentional sharing (pillar-colour flags + 'Shared with' plaque). Undeclared cross-pillar use = flags + amber '!' + Registry health suggestion. A direct bridge is shown either way.
- Messaging reality (Dan's diagram, 2026-10-24): one 'Stratus' namespace in the multi-tenant core credit platform. Per-service outbox topic (<pillar>-<svc>-outbox-topic), chains via subscription auto-forward. Intra-pillar: subscription -> consumer queue. Egress: egress subs -> sbt-<pillar>-egress topic (+ Egress Service via <pillar>-egrs-inbound-queue). Ingress: other pillar owns <pillar>-igrs-<from>-egress sub -> its ingress topic -> service inbox queues. Messaging is hub-and-spoke too. Idea: discover chains via ForwardTo and ownership by name prefix, with a messagingCode per system. (Resolved later in this round: see the messaging entries below and in the plan.)
- Real SB naming (Dan screenshots, NDT namespace sb-ndt-northeurope...): topics {pillar}-{svc}-outbox (cl-mgs-outbox); pillar ingress topic {pillar}-igrs-outbox with one sub per pillar service; subs named {consumerPillar}-{consumerSvc}-{source}-subscription (cl-ss-mgs-outbox-subscription internal, frd-fss-cl-mgs-outbox-subscription cross-pillar direct); *-cl-egress-subscription forward to egress. Pillar codes seen: cl, ams, frd, rp. Proposed: discover by convention (patterns in town.yaml), ids reuse codes (cl.mgs). Asked whether direct cross-pillar subscriptions are allowed or legacy.
- Naming convention applies to ALL multi-tenant (core credit) SB entities. Single-tenant: declared in pillar files until their convention is known. Central egress service sends to external parties (mostly Snowflake for the data team): draw it on the hub with a bridge to a Snowflake vendor island.
- CORRECTION: the intended cross-pillar pattern is a DIRECT subscription to the other pillar's outbox topic. The egress topic is only for external parties + legacy. Pillar-to-pillar via egress/ingress = legacy: draw it with an amber pennant and list it in Registry health as migration candidates.
- Message types: not declared (they live in subscription filters). Optional later: read the filter rules to show them in the drawer. Non-publishing systems: no pier. Kafka/external publishing: later a second port kind on the hub. Until then, an optional publishesExternal: {via: kafka, target: ext.x} in the pillar file.
- S5 Messaging ownership: DISCOVER BY CONVENTION (Dan tapped). Read entities from SB, parse names with patterns in town.yaml, ids reuse the codes (cl.mgs), pillar files declare only exceptions (single-tenant, external). Unparseable entities go to Lost & found.
- S6 Namespaces: a namespace shared by several pillars = a port on the hub; one used by a single pillar = a small harbour on that island. Queues = direct boat route to the consumer pier (no decision needed).
- S8 Third parties: third-parties.yaml with host patterns matched against dependency targets (AppDependencies.Target / server.address). Unknown external hosts suggested in Registry health.
All sources/hub/messaging/third-party decisions written into the plan doc (rev 20, 2026-10-24).
- Generality (Dan's concern 2026-10-24): generic core (groups, systems, resources, links sync/async/external, brokers, vendors, link flags). Company specifics only in config/adapters: groupLabel (Pillar), ownership tags, naming regexes, topology: hub-and-spoke|direct, legacy rules as flags. Guardrail: the Phase 1 demo ships two fake towns (company-like + a different one: teams, direct calls, Kafka, no gateway). Asked Dan: internal only or possibly OSS/product?
- Dan's goal (2026-10-24): built in his own time; give to his company, maybe open source later or take to a future employer. So: app repo company-neutral (sample registries only), real company registry in a separate private repo/folder (revises decision 1, pending Dan's objection). No company names/patterns/screenshots in public code. Suggested he check his employment IP clause and pick a licence early.
- Repo model (Dan, 2026-10-24): Dan's repo = generic app + samples/ (two fake towns), Registry:Path setting. Company forks and adds only config/ and optional extensions/, never editing core, so pulling upstream merges cleanly. Possibly later: a published container + mounted config.

## Round 3: Phase 1

Started 2026-10-24 after the architecture/config rounds (observability-city-architecture, observability-city-plan).
Topics: 1 sample towns, 2 generator realism, 3 scenario switcher UX, 4 art style, 5 building models source, 6 landscape, 7 camera/controls, 8 UI chrome/legend, 9 performance target, 10 build slices.
- 1 Sample towns: 'Galactic Credit' (14 pillars: cl, acc, pay, frd, cus, acq, dec, bil, col, dsp, rwd, obk, ntf, dat; hub = APIM, SB port, central egress, shared customer DB; vendors: credit bureau, card network, SMS/email, Snowflake; ~40 systems; deliberate legacy egress routes + an undeclared shared DB) and 'Droid Shop' (teams Web, Mobile, Inventory, Checkout, Shipping, Notifications; Kafka port; topology direct; vendors: payment gateway, courier, push). Top-bar town switcher. Names were Dan's choice; the pillar list is my first cut.
- 2 Generator baseline: gentle noise + daily traffic cycle (speedable, e.g. a 'day' in 2 min), autoscaling floors at peak. Plus a 'calm' toggle to freeze it flat. Scenarios layer on top of the baseline.
- 3 Scenario UX: stackable + story player. Left panel 'Break things': scenario button then click a target in the town/search, active chips with an intensity slider, Reset. Bottom 'Stories' bar: play/pause/speed/scrub, step captions, camera glides. Stories = YAML timelines of the same effects (steps at/caption/apply/clear/ramp), sent to the Fake generator. 'Record' clicks into a story may come after Phase 1.
- 4 Art style: built a live comparison artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/KkAR3joVpBjCkC3aS1Fv6Q (three.js 0.160, A dusk w/ bloom, B day, C toon OutlineEffect). Source: previews/1-art-style-and-lighting.html
- 4 DECIDED: lighting presets over the same models. Day is the default, with a Day/Dusk toggle in the top bar (remembered per browser). Auto by local time (+ maybe Night for wall screens) later. Day gets beacon halos / bigger beacons for readability. Toon dropped (different pipeline). Final art must be game-quality polish; plan an early look-dev slice (one island at final quality).
- 5 DECIDED: free CC0 kits (Kenney + KayKit/Quaternius) plus custom touches (beacon, DLQ pen, signs). Themes replace buildings.yaml: themes/{name}/theme.yaml maps building kinds to models with anchors (beacon roof point, floor module height, pier attach), day/dusk presets, optional wording; runtime theme picker. Phase 1 ships one default theme 'harbour-town'; space/city/medieval later. Commercial packs (Dan may buy) live only in a private fork/git-ignored folder, never in the public repo.
- 6 DECIDED: organic generated coastlines (seeded from pillar id, stable), with a town centre of plots inside (Dan's idea, ordered blocks): one building per plot, spare plots as lawn, trees/hill around. Preview https://claude.ai/artifact/622xiVYcuCpTVtkbaudfar. Plots: square plots + streets (A1). Dan liked both and said pick whichever works best with city kits, so square was chosen (kits are square-footprint; streets carry walkers and repair vans).
- 7 DECIDED: free orbit camera (OrbitControls), tilt limited ~25-75°, zoom/pan bounds, never under water. Click/search glides to a building and opens the drawer; any drag cancels a glide; Home button frames the town. Labels only for selected/troubled. Wall-display mode auto-tours troubled islands.
- 8 DECIDED: UI layout A (mockup https://claude.ai/artifact/MNj7Dsx1MKbDpgZcnTXCVA). Top bar: town + env switchers, town totals (req/s, errors, p99, DLQ, incidents), search, Day/Dusk, theme picker, legend (?), Home. Left: collapsible 'Break things' panel (demo only). Right: inspector side drawer (status, key numbers, tabs Overview/Links/Logs/Config with value origins). Bottom: story bar with captions. Legend card opens on first visit, then behind '?'.
- 9 DECIDED: game-style quality presets Low/Medium/High/Ultra + Wall (30 fps cap for TVs/mini PCs). Auto: initial guess from GPU string/screen/memory, then measure frame time and step down/up with hysteresis (r3f drei PerformanceMonitor + AdaptiveDpr). Manual override in settings, remembered per browser. Target: smooth on an ordinary office laptop at Medium; dev PCs with GPUs get High/Ultra. Presets toggle shadows, bloom, water effects, pixel ratio, walker count, far-detail distance.
- 10 DECIDED: thin slice first, then widen. Slice 1 = hub + one island at final art quality, live fake data via the real pipeline (contract, generator, SignalR), 3 signals. Then add signals, islands, UI, scenarios/stories, second town in demoable slices.
