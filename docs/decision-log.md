# Decision log

How each decision in the plan was reached, round by round, with Dan on 24 Oct 2026. The plan document has the final version of every decision. This log keeps the extra detail and reasoning that a builder may need. Entries are in the order they were made, so a later entry can replace an earlier one (for example, early notes about districts, a depot and shared-infra.yaml were replaced by islands per pillar, the hub and hub.yaml). When this log and the plan disagree, the plan wins.

## Round 1: visual language

Decisions Dan made on 2026-10-24, in the planning thread:
- Weather = incidents only. Metrics drive buildings, not the sky.
- Roads: service-to-depot spokes (Service Bus = central depot), walkways only for direct sync calls, entry paths from the edge for outside callers.
- Third parties: their own area, health inferred from errors and latency on our calls to them, with pedestrian walks to them.
- Demo scenario switcher: one button per scenario (healthy, DLQ, high traffic, slow, incident, many errors, down, third-party outage), and also a detailed custom mode (set values per service).
- Demo data: registry files for teams and systems loaded as normal. Only the data sources are faked, as a fake adapter.
- Incidents: Grafana IRM, details to be worked out later.
- Backend: .NET preferred (Dan's strength), unless there's a strong reason otherwise.
- Runs locally on a developer PC for now. No SSO. Hosting in Azure comes later.

- Rendering: 3D low-poly with Three.js/react-three-fiber. Frontend in TypeScript, backend ASP.NET Core + SignalR.
- DLQs: a loading dock and red skip beside each subscriber, in its team's district. The depot only shows a warning light.
- Team layout: districts ring the depot. Systems declare calls/publishes/subscribes by system id, team closeness is computed from those links, and layout.yaml can pin positions.

Plan doc (2026-10-24): https://claude.ai/artifact/9GzxXsugoyXUQ8NoVc5Rou. Phases: 1 demo with fake sources, 2 Grafana/Prometheus + Log Analytics, 3 Service Bus/DLQs, 4 IRM incidents + third parties, 5 trustworthy, 6 host and grow. Waiting on Dan's review before any code.

**Why:** these steer the phased build plan. **How to apply:** don't re-ask them, and build the plan on top of them.

Visual language review, one signal at a time (started 2026-10-24, Dan said the first table copied the brief too closely):
- 1 System: shape by role. Role is optional in YAML, a closed enum, inferred from links/telemetry when omitted (api = receives requests, worker = only messaging, api+messaging = tower with dock, db/cache from dependency type, cron must be declared). The drift report flags contradictions.
- 2 Team: island per team. A central hub island (platform-owned, shared-infra.yaml) holds Service Bus and shared DBs, caches and APIM. Shared = owner platform, or referenced by 2+ teams (with a warning). Mainland coast = outside callers; third parties on small offshore islands.
- 3 Messaging: Service Bus = port on the hub island. Cargo boats sail to each subscriber team's pier; backlog = crates stacking on the pier. Water = async, bridges = sync (walkers).
- 4 DLQ: red quarantine pen on the team pier fills with broken crates, a red lighthouse beam flashes when >0, plus a floating count badge ("DLQ 37"). The hub port shows the town-wide total.
- 5 Traffic/capacity: floors = instance count on a doubling scale (1,2,3-4,5-8,9-16,17-32,33-64,65+ = 8 floors, the cap), exact count on a plaque. Lit windows = load (rps). Unhealthy instances = dark or cracked floors. Height changes are eased.
- 6 Errors: rooftop beacon (glowing bulb on a mast mid-roof, light pool on the roof), green/amber/red, red pulses, minimum on-screen size. Defaults 1% amber and 5% red, overridable per system. Windows weren't visible enough from afar for errors.
- 7 Latency/movement: number of walkers/boats = traffic, their speed = latency, a queue at the door/pier grows when p99 > threshold, and the crowd turns angry (stamping, amber "!" bubble, never red) when over SLA (slaP99Ms per system, default 1 s). Red stays reserved for errors.
- 8 Down: blackout, grey, barriers on the bridge, walkers turn back, a "Closed for repairs" sign and a repair van. Partial: the share of dark/cracked floors (top down) = the share of unhealthy instances, min 1 floor if any are unhealthy, plaque "5/10 healthy". Stale = grey, never healthy.
- 9 Incident: weather over the assigned team's island only (IRM assigns incidents to teams). sev4 light cloud, sev3 overcast, sev2 rain, sev1 storm with lightning. Worst severity wins, plus a count on the island sign. The rest of the town stays sunny. Platform incidents go over the hub. irmTeam is set in the team YAML. No building marker for now.
- 10 Outside callers: an airport on the map edge (plane rate = total inbound, arrivals board shows req/s). Passengers walk over main bridges only to islands that have external systems. Config: team-level `external: true` default with per-system override. Queue and anger show at the bridge end.
- 11 Sync calls (proposed mix, Dan's company is hub and spoke via APIM): each island has one bridge to the hub, with APIM as a gateway building on the hub (with its own signals). Walkers carry a team-colour flag (palette without red/amber/green). A dependency overlay shows dashed arcs team to team, sized by volume. Calls that bypass APIM get a direct bridge, which flags rule breaks. Data from APIM gateway logs or traces.
- 12 Third parties: small offshore islands with a direct bridge from each calling island. Same beacon/queue/closed rules, inferred from our outbound calls.
All 12 signals were written into the plan doc's visual language table on 2026-10-24. Dan didn't explicitly confirm the #11 hub-and-spoke mix (it was proposed, then he moved on).

## Round 2: architecture, config, sources, hub and messaging

Started 2026-10-24, after the visual language review (observability-city-plan). 14 topics: registry location, solution shape, live push, snapshot contract, adapter model, fake source/scenarios, local Azure auth, history storage, file layout/ownership, id naming, sources mapping, defaults/overrides, layout file, scenario files.

- 1 Registry lives in the app repo (registry/ folder). The loader takes a folder path, so it can move to a separate repo later.
- 2 Solution: .NET Aspire AppHost orchestrating the Host (API + SignalR), Core, Adapters.*, Contracts, and the frontend/ Vite app. azd to Azure Container Apps later.
- 3 Live push: SignalR (groups for per-team/log tail, two-way, Azure SignalR scale-out). The transport stays behind an interface, so it's swappable. Dan wasn't fully sure, so it's low stakes.
- 4 Contract: C# Snapshot records are the source of truth. The build emits openapi.json for /api/snapshot/latest, and openapi-typescript generates contracts.ts, so drift is a build error. Plus golden snapshot fixtures per scenario, used by both C# and TS renderer tests. (Dan was between this and hand-written types plus a contract test.)
- 5 Adapters: poll per source (own BackgroundService loop, interval, timeout, retry). One batched query for all systems per poll. A failure stales only that source. No webhooks while it runs on a dev PC.
- 6 Fake/scenarios: both. A generator adapter (healthy noisy baseline + scenario effects layered on top, sliders, can stand in for unwired systems) and a replay mode playing fixture timelines for tests and scripted demos (later reused for history replay).
- 7 Azure auth: DefaultAzureCredential (your own az/VS login locally, managed identity when hosted). Grafana/IRM tokens in dotnet user-secrets locally and Key Vault when hosted, never in YAML.
- 8 History: PARKED by Dan (2026-10-24). Live data only for the first versions. Later options: Cosmos everywhere (Aspire emulator, native TTL), SQLite, configurable retention (e.g. 90 days) with downsampling after 7 days (~5 GB vs ~23 GB full res).
- 9 Files: one file per pillar (registry/pillars/<pillar>.yaml) plus shared files (sources, shared-infra, messaging, third-parties, layout). Folder-per-team support can come later if a big team complains.
- Ownership data (Dan, 2026-10-24): the core credit platform (multi-tenant) has an Azure tag identifying the pillar/domain and a `pillar.code` telemetry attribute. Single-tenant parts of the estate may have neither. Plan: town.yaml `ownership: {azureTag: pillar, telemetryAttribute: pillar.code}`. Untagged systems go to a "Lost & found" island. Discovery writes drafts to registry/_discovered/ and never edits team files. A Registry health panel shows drift with fix lines.
- 10 Islands = PILLARS (not teams), one file per pillar, ids <pillarcode>.<system> e.g. crd.ledger-api, auto-placed from Azure tag and pillar.code. IRM teams map to pillars. Team districts within islands possible later. Validation: prefix must match the file, kebab-case, unique, aliases on rename, schema autocomplete, "did you mean".
- 11 Source mapping: convention + overrides. Default queries per source type on standard OTel metrics / App Insights tables joined by service name. A system adds a custom PromQL/KQL only when it's non-standard. Pillar default source, system can override.
- 12 Defaults: registry/defaults.yaml (app-wide per role: API 1 s, worker 30 s, DB 100 ms, errors 1%/5%, DLQ >0; same values baked into code as a last resort) -> pillar file "defaults" per role -> system override. Dan wants app-wide fallbacks when a pillar forgets its defaults. Example: an orchestrator API with an SLA of 2 s vs quick internal APIs at 300 ms. The drawer shows each effective value and its origin.
- 13 Layout: deterministic, no drag/pin. layout.yaml has islands.order (validated pillar ids; missing pillars appended with a warning). Ring around the hub clockwise from the top, auto radius. Above one ring, a staggered second ring (its bridges pass between first-ring islands). Ring size is `ringCapacity` in layout.yaml (default 12, 6 to 18); at most 2 rings, so at most (ringCapacity x 2) - 1 pillars (23 at 12, 35 at 18), both enforced by the loader, validator and snapshot guard. Buildings are gridded in pillar-file order. Fixed: hub centre, airport in the second ring's north slot, vendors outside the ring road, Lost & found corner. Demo uses ~14 fake pillars.
- 14 Scenarios: registry/scenarios/*.yaml (caption + effects with target/effect/intensity, optional timeline), validated like the registry. Custom mode Save writes YAML, and the buttons are generated from the files.
All 14 topics written into the plan doc on 2026-10-24 (architecture decisions table + Registry config section rewritten for pillars).

## Slice 1 look, round 2 (Dan, 2026-10-24)
- Requests are cars, not pedestrians: sedan = internal, taxi = external, red hatchback = failed. Count follows request rate, speed follows p99 against the SLA. Replaces walkers wherever the plan says walkers.
- Buildings read as a town (brick, trim, awnings, pitched roofs), windows are square and sized in metres, signs stand on a single post on land.
- A service is an API, a worker and a database placed side by side (registry `service` field). Each pillar island has a port.
- Roads, cars and street props come from the CC0 KayKit City Builder Bits kit.

## Slice 1 look, round 3 (Dan, 2026-10-24)
- Layout: no more two rows facing a central street (fronts hidden). Each service is a pad (API, worker and DB side by side, fronts toward a road), pads sit on a grid of rows along side roads off one spine road from the gate. Lone systems share a pad. Islands are sized to fit their grid, so a pillar with 5 services is about three rows by two columns.
- Every building has a name and "N/M healthy" board (fades out when far, always on when troubled).
- Distant windows blend to the wall's average colour instead of shimmering.
- Bridges: stone and concrete road bridge with parapets, lamps and gate posts. Each island has one radial spoke road and bridge to the hub, so bridges never cross. Calls between islands go through the hub (hub and spoke), and the dependency overlay shows team-to-team links as arcs rather than extra bridges. Beyond about 12 islands use a second, staggered ring (already in the plan). Bridges are cheap to render (instanced road tiles); the limit is readability, not performance.
- Second sample island: Payments (two services).

## Slice 1 look, round 4 (Dan, 2026-10-24)
- More cars (spawn rate scales with request rate, with a minimum gap so queues do not pile up) and boats for messaging between island ports and the hub.
- Five sample islands around the hub (Core Ledger, Payments, Fraud, Customer, Collections) to see how several islands connect. Islands are evenly spaced on a ring and each gets one radial bridge. Spokes are nudged off the hub's axes.
- Wider lawn between building rows so fronts are never hidden by the row in front.
- Worker is a factory and database is a storage-units building.

Thresholds (`resources`, `throttle`) cascade from defaults.yaml like the existing ones. Metrics are nullable: null means not reported, never zero. High resource use gives amber, throttling gives a red beacon and a lowered barrier gate.
- New fake effects: High CPU, High memory, DB saturated.

## Slice 1 look, round 5 (Dan, 2026-10-24)
- All cars are the same size: each model is scaled to one length, and the grow-in at route ends is gone.
- Boats stay on water. A bridge is a low road deck, so a boat cannot pass under it, and a lane round the hub would cross every bridge. Instead each island has a quay on the hub in the water gap beside its bridge, and a lane over open sea (grid search around land and bridges, scripts in frontend/src/scene/sea.ts) to the island's harbour.

## Data rule: metrics first (Dan, 2026-10-24)
- Health and every visual come from metrics, never from logs or traces, because companies often sample those heavily. Adapters read pre-aggregated platform or OpenTelemetry metrics (not sampled log-based counts).
- Traces or dependency data may only help discover which systems call which (existence of a link, not counts). Links stay declared in the registry first.
- Logs are an on-demand view in a building's panel and never decide a colour.
- Missing data shows as unknown (null), never as healthy.

## Slice 1 look, round 6 (Dan, 2026-10-25)
- Lights come on floor by floor from the ground up: CPU for apps and workers, percent of provisioned capacity (RU style) for databases.
- Every building has a service lane beside the road, three cars long, so a queue never clogs the island road. Dwell time at the door is the latency. SLA breach shows as the queue, amber hazard lights, a "!" honk bubble and a p99 against SLA line on the name board. A car that finds the lane full turns round, counted by a "+N waiting" marker. Served cars drive into the building (no return trip, which also removed most junction jams).
- Failed requests are cars with a red flag on the roof, turned away at the door and driving back. Colour stays internal blue or external yellow, and the red hatchback is gone.
- Cars are a sample, not a count (a few cars stand for many requests; README notes it). Name board gets ERR and SLA rows.
- Errors mean 5xx, timeouts and unhandled exceptions. 4xx are not failures. 429 is throttling.
- Boats are smaller and their crates show load. A backlog is crates piled on the island quay with a "waiting N, oldest" sign (queue depth and oldest message age, from broker metrics). Dead letters are a pen of broken crates with a "DLQ N" badge and a sweeping red light. The hub shows the total DLQ. New contract fields: queue depth, oldest message seconds, dead letters, backlog thresholds (amber 60 s, red 300 s, cascading).
- Cars never overlap. Rules: in a lane brake for the car ahead whatever its age. At crossings and merges a car keeps out of any older car's body and swept path (four points ahead) and out of any younger car's body, so waiting mostly depends on older cars. Known limit: when two cars still end up waiting for each other for over a second and a half, the younger one dissolves from the sample. About one car in seven does this at the busiest junctions in the sample town. A reservation scheme at junctions would remove that and is the next step if it is noticeable.

## Slice 1 finishing changes, round 7 (Dan, 2026-10-25)
- A full service lane turns nobody round (that read as a rejection). Cars stop appearing for that building instead, so the road stays clear. The lane holds five cars where it is long enough (three beside the spine). The "+N waiting" marker is now the requests in flight (rate times about half of p99) that the lane has no room to draw. Turning round is only for failed requests.
- Same-pillar calls follow declared links. A system lists the ids it calls under `calls` in its pillar file, the loader checks every id exists and is not itself, and the snapshot carries `calls` for each node. Cars drive from the caller's door to the callee's lane only along those links. Until a source reports a link's own call rate, the callee's rate is shared evenly between its declared callers. No declared caller means no same-island traffic for that system. All five sample pillars now declare their calls.
- Junction standoffs: the sampled run drops from about one car in seven dissolving to about one in twenty (fewer cars, since traffic now follows declared links). A reservation scheme at junctions is still the proper fix if the remaining ones are noticed.

## Slice 2: all islands (2026-10-25)
- The sample town is the full Galactic Credit: 14 pillars (cl, acc, pay, frd, cus, acq, dec, bil, col, dsp, rwd, obk, ntf, dat) and a Lost & found island, about 110 systems, the hub's four shared resources, and four outside callers declared in `hub.yaml` (`externalCallers`, each may only call systems marked external). Every pillar declares `calls` between its own systems.
- `layout.yaml` lists the islands in the order they stand round the hub. Pillars it does not list follow in file order, and Lost & found is always last. A ring holds 12 islands. More stand in a staggered second ring, each in the gap between two first-ring islands with its bridge passing through the gap, and the first ring spaces out to leave that room.
- The hub grows with the island count so every spoke road has room where it meets the coast. Spokes can not start where a hub building stands, so they share the free arcs between the four buildings' axes in island order. A bridge slants a little where its spoke had to move off its island's direction.
- Lost & found collects systems that carry no pillar tag and no `pillar.code`. In the sample it is a normal pillar file (`pillars/lost-found.yaml`); discovery will write there later. It is drawn like any island with a heap of boxes and bins by the gate.
- Registry validation reports every problem at once, each naming its file. Ids must be `pillar.system`, the prefix matching the file, kebab-case and unique. Unknown keys, roles, call targets and layout entries get a "did you mean" suggestion. `aliases` keep old ids working in other files after a rename, and calls to an alias resolve to the current id.
- Editor schemas live in `<registry>/schema/`. The structure schemas ship in the app, and `system-ids.schema.json` lists the registry's own ids so `calls` and `layout.yaml` autocomplete. `dotnet run --project src/ObservabilityCity.Host -- --write-schema` refreshes them, and a test fails when they are out of date.
- Camera: clicking a building (not dragging) or picking a search result glides to it and keeps its name board visible. Search covers islands and buildings, and `/` focuses it. Home works out the distance from the coast points of every island so the whole town fits, and the zoom and pan limits grow with the town.
- Performance at 15 islands and 109 buildings: a sea-lane search that recomputed its grid for every island froze the browser, so the grid is now built once and the search settles each cell once. Scenery that never changes (roads, pads, lamps, props, bridges, quays) is merged into a few meshes per island, which took draw calls from about 8,900 to about 4,700. Quality steps down on its own when the average frame takes longer than 33 ms: first shadows refresh a few times a second at a lower resolution, then shadows turn off. It never steps back up by itself. `?quality=high|medium|low` pins a level. The buildings themselves are still about 45 meshes each (hidden floor modules included) and are the next thing to merge if a real GPU struggles.
- Goldens: the calm town and a bad day across several islands as snapshots, and the island layout (positions, rings, sizes) as a TypeScript golden.
- Other signals (slice 4): incidents arrive through `IIncidentSource` (fake now, Grafana IRM later) and show as weather over the assigned island only; a count goes on the island sign and the top bar lists islands with open incidents.
- Third parties live in `third-parties.yaml` (id, name, `calledBy`, SLA, optional thresholds). Their health is inferred from the calls our systems make to them, never polled. A ring road circles the whole town outside the last island ring. Each calling island has a back-gate bridge to the road, and the vendors stand outside the road on short spurs, spread round it near their callers. Vendor calls never go via the hub. An island whose back-gate bridge is blocked shows overlay arcs only.
- The airport takes a reserved slot in the second ring (north) with an ordinary spoke to the hub gateway, so it always has a bridge; outside taxis start at the gateway. Plane rate follows the sampled external request rate.
- `gatewayCalls` on a system lists cross-pillar calls that go through the gateway. They count for the dependency overlay and are the only way a hub-and-spoke town gets team-to-team arcs.
- Dependency overlay: dashed arcs in the caller's colour, thickness by volume, dashes marching from caller to callee. Off until switched on in the top bar.
- Stale data turns a building grey; the top bar shows a stale count.
- Messaging (slice 3): a pillar file (and `hub.yaml`) has an optional `messaging` block. `topics` lists the services whose outbox the pillar publishes, each becoming the topic `{pillar}-{service}-outbox` (`hub-{name}` for the hub). `subscriptions` lists `topic` and `consumer` (a worker of the same pillar). Pillars subscribe directly to each other's outboxes. Reading the hub's egress topic (`hub-egress`) is the old route: it must say `legacy: true` (and legacy is allowed nowhere else) and is drawn with an amber pennant on the pier. Egress to external parties is a vendor call, not a subscription. Messaging is declared in the registry for now (the fake source stands in); discovery from Service Bus names comes with the real adapter.
- Link rule (Dan): connectors follow real links only. A pier, its board and its sea lane exist only for an island that publishes a topic or has a subscribing worker. A bridge to the hub exists only for an island with an API, or a call link over its coast in either direction (a call to a hub database or cache, a `gatewayCalls`, a call into it from another island). An island with neither stands alone and the registry warns (a warning, never an error). Buildings exist only for real roles: no database means no storage unit, no worker no factory. The backend works the rule out once (`TownLinks`) and sends `hasBridge` and `messaging` on every island, so the renderer and the registry cannot disagree.
- Only a worker with a subscription has a queue and a dead-letter queue (the fake source and the pier both follow this). The town-wide dead-letter total is `totals.deadLetters`; the hub lighthouse beside the Service Bus port and a top-bar chip show it. The port's own sign counts topics, subscriptions and piers.
- Sample town: Reconciliation (`rcn`) is worker-only (a pier, no bridge) and Regulatory Reporting (`reg`) has an API and a cache that reads the shared hub database (a bridge, no pier, no worker, no database of its own). `shared-test-data/registries/links-town` has one island for each case and is what the backend, frontend and browser tests of the rule run on.

## Slice 3.5: environments, tenants, service groups (2026-10-26)
- Environments are files: `environments/{dev,staging,prod}.yaml` hold the tenants, the sources and the exclusions of one environment, and pillar and system files are shared by all of them. One backend serves every environment, each with its own source, latest readings and snapshot stream; SignalR groups clients by environment. `?env=` picks one; nothing asked gets the default; a name that does not exist gets an `environmentUnknown` message and no snapshots (a typo must not land anyone on production). A town without environment files has one, `demo`.
- An environment's `exclude` drops systems, and the calls, subscriptions, topics and vendor links that hang on them; an island left with nothing is not drawn. Hub resources cannot be excluded.
- A database with `tenantPattern: "merchant-{tenant}-db"` has a database for each tenant: a storage yard with a unit for each tenant on the service's pad, a sign "N tenants, M throttled", and its own utilisation and throttling per tenant. A tenant found in the readings but not listed is `unknown` (purple, "?"). Test tenants are paler and striped and never go past amber: they cannot turn a store, its service or the town red. The store's own numbers are the worst of its customer and unknown tenants.
- Cars carry a tenant, picked by share (from the tenant databases' request rates; even until they report, with a test tenant counting a tenth of a customer). Dan: the "Observability City" title leaves the top bar, the environment name becomes a dropdown, and next to it is a tenant dropdown (not just a search box, because tenant ids are often GUIDs nobody remembers): display name first with the id beside it, groups for customers, test and unknown tenants, search by name or id, multiselect, a dot on tenants in trouble, and a Hide test tenants switch. Following several tenants dims the rest and draws only their cars. Slice 5 builds the rest of the top bar around these.
- Services: systems group by the `service:` field, not by file order, one pad each (three systems side by side, more take more pads). The pad has a low sign with the service's name and worst state and a hedge round it; clicking either opens a service panel. Systems with no service stand on a pad of their own. The validator warns when a service is split across pillars.
- Fake source: `Saturate` can be aimed at one tenant with `store#tenant` (or `*#tenant`); no other effect accepts that.
